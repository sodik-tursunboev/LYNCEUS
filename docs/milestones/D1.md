# D1: Page detonation (open the page in a sandbox and judge what it shows)

**Status:** approved by the owner on 2026-11-11 (download, live pages, Docker); **built and measured the same day.** Results at the end and in `docs/eval/detonate.md`; the decision record is ADR-025.

## Why

E1 (`docs/eval/url-real.md`): on **91.2%** of 133,195 real phishing URLs no URL rule fires at all. The address alone says nothing; the page says everything: a Microsoft login form on `xyz.top`, a form posting passwords to a Telegram bot, a page that hides from developer tools. D1 opens the page in a throwaway, locked-down browser and turns what it sees into signals, combined with the URL rules and the lists by the same scoring model (ADR-013).

## Scope (D1)

1. **Detonation container** `infra/detonator/`: `FROM mcr.microsoft.com/playwright/python:v1.64.1-noble` (pinned by digest after the first pull), `playwright==1.64.1` installed with `--require-hashes` (the image ships browsers, not the package), `detonate.py` reads one JSON request on stdin and writes one JSON result on stdout.
2. **Following Playwright's own guidance for untrusted sites:** browsers run as the non-root `pwuser` with Chromium's sandbox **on**, under Playwright's seccomp profile. Playwright's documentation states its image is not meant for visiting untrusted websites as shipped; the hardening below is why D1 does it anyway.
3. **Container hardening:** `docker run --rm -i --user pwuser --read-only --tmpfs /tmp --cap-drop ALL --security-opt no-new-privileges --security-opt seccomp=<profile> --memory 1g --cpus 1.5 --pids-limit 512 --shm-size 1g --init`, its own Docker network, no volumes, no published ports, killed at 30 s wall clock (`docker kill`), at most 2 at once.
4. **Egress guard inside the container:** Chromium goes through a small in-container forward proxy (standard library) that resolves every name itself and refuses anything that is not a public address on port 80 or 443: private, loopback, link-local, CGNAT, cloud metadata, multicast, `host.docker.internal`. QUIC off, WebRTC limited to proxied traffic. The page cannot reach your network, your router, the Docker host or the database container.
5. **What it records (bounded, never more):** final URL and redirect chain (20 hops), title, visible text (20,000 characters), forms (action, method, input types and names; never values), password and email fields, frames and their origins, script hosts, hosts contacted (100), favicon hash, the main page's TLS certificate dates, attempted downloads (blocked; type and extension only), anti-analysis markers, and one screenshot (JPEG, 1280×800, 300 KB at most).
6. **What it never does:** type, click, submit, grant a permission, keep a download, or run longer than 30 s. Dialogs are dismissed.
7. **Host side** `core/detonate/`: runs the container (argument list, no shell), validates the reply against a closed pydantic schema with size caps, re-encodes the screenshot in the existing AppContainer sandbox (Pillow) before anything displays it.
8. **Page rules** `rules/page/` (new pack, ruleset 1.0.0, weights in `scoring.toml`), first set:

   | Rule | Fires when | Category |
   |---|---|---|
   | `page.brand_credential` | the page names or shows a brand (title, text, logo alt, favicon) and asks for a password or email, on a host that brand does not own | impersonation |
   | `page.form_offsite` | a credential form posts to another registered domain, a raw IP, or a known collection endpoint (Telegram bot API, form-relay services) | exfiltration |
   | `page.credential_form` | a password field on a host that is not an official domain (weak alone) | credential |
   | `page.hosting_platform_login` | a credential form on free or shared hosting (pages.dev, workers.dev, web.app, github.io, IPFS gateways, site builders, tunnels) | hosting |
   | `page.redirected_offsite` | the page you land on is on another site; the final URL also goes through the URL rules | redirect |
   | `page.evasion` | developer-tools detection, right-click or keyboard blocking, `navigator.webdriver` checks, a login form built by heavily obfuscated script | evasion |
   | `page.smuggling` | the page starts a download built from a blob or data URL | delivery |

9. **Entry points:** `python -m core.cli detonate <url> [--json]`, `POST /v1/urls/detonate` (on demand, its own small rate limit), and an **"Open in sandbox"** button on the console's Analyze page that shows the screenshot and the page findings next to the URL verdict.
10. **Measurement** `python -m eval.detonate`: fresh OpenPhish and PhishTank URLs (about 300 live phishing pages), Tranco sites (about 300) and real login pages of listed brands (about 100). Reported: caught by the URL alone vs with the page, false alarms on legitimate pages, pages already dead (reported apart, not as misses or catches), Wilson intervals. URLs stay in `.data/` (outside git); only counts are published.

## Not in D1

- **Automatic detonation** while browsing or for email links (D1b, opt-in): a second visit can use up a one-time link (password reset, magic sign-in), and opening a link from an email tells its sender that your address is read.
- The local vision model naming the brand on the screenshot (D1b, advice only, like the macro reviewer).
- Certificate-transparency and newly-registered-domain feeds; a separate virtual machine for detonation.

## Security and privacy implications

- **Real phishing pages are opened from your internet connection.** The attacker's server sees your public IP and a Linux Chromium visiting. For the measurement this is research traffic; on demand it is your own click. Nothing is typed or sent to the page.
- **A browser exploit** would have to break Chromium's sandbox, then the container (no capabilities, seccomp, read-only, non-root), and would then be inside Docker Desktop's Linux VM, not Windows. **Residual risk:** that VM also runs the PostgreSQL container; a container escape could reach it. A dedicated VM is the fix, deferred.
- **What comes back** is data checked against a closed schema; the screenshot is re-encoded in the parser sandbox. The screenshot can show whatever the page shows; it is displayed once and **not stored**. No URL text is stored (decision D2 holds); page findings keep hosts and rule ids only.
- **Failure is never "safe":** if the page cannot be opened (dead, timeout, Docker off), the verdict is the URL-only verdict with "page not analysed: <reason>".

## Acceptance criteria

1. The image builds from a pinned digest; Chromium runs as `pwuser` with its sandbox on (tested).
2. Egress tests from inside the container: private, loopback, link-local, metadata, `host.docker.internal`, IPv6 loopback, a DNS name that resolves to a private address and non-web ports are all refused.
3. Each page rule fires on its synthetic page and not on its benign twin (pages served locally inside the container, no internet).
4. Limits hold: a page that never finishes, an endless redirect loop, alert loops, a huge DOM and a download flood all end with a bounded result inside 30 s, and the core never hangs.
5. Page signals combine with URL and intel signals through the existing scoring; MALICIOUS still needs two independent strong categories; any failure gives the URL-only verdict with the reason.
6. CLI, API and the Analyze page button work end to end on this machine.
7. The live measurement runs once and is published with intervals, whatever the number is.
8. All existing suites pass; lint and types clean; ADR-025, ADR-004 (dependencies), FEATURES, TECHNICAL_SPEC, PRIVACY and KNOWN_LIMITATIONS updated.

## What was built (2026-11-11)

- `infra/detonator/` (Dockerfile, `detonate.py`, `egress.py`, hash-pinned `requirements.txt`, Playwright's seccomp profile), `python tasks.py detonator`.
- `core/detonate/` (`runner.py`: networks, proxy, one container per page, output cap, kill; `models.py`: the closed schema; `judge.py`: page signals plus the landing address, scored with everything else), `rules/page/` (rules, `scoring.toml`, `lists.toml`, `brands.toml`).
- `POST /v1/urls/detonate`, `python -m core.cli detonate`, the console's "Open in sandbox" panel with the screenshot, `python -m eval.detonate`.
- Tests: 33 page-rule tests, 42 egress and hardening tests, 4 API tests, 12 tests against the real container (sandbox on, no route out, six private targets refused, a public page through the proxy, the fixture pages), 1 console test.

## Where it differs from the plan

- **`SYS_CHROOT` is given back to the browser container.** Chromium's sandbox chroots itself inside a user namespace, and Docker's seccomp profile allows `chroot` only to a container holding that capability. The browser runs as `pwuser`, so it has no effective capabilities; the alternative (Chromium without its sandbox) is worse.
- **No rule for "a password field" or "moved to another site"** (planned as `page.credential_form` and `page.redirected_offsite`): next to `url.login_path` (0.4) any weight at all pushes ordinary login pages over 45. The landing address goes through the URL rules instead.
- **Added after looking at the dev half only:** a payment-card form counts as a credential ask (card-verification and parcel-fee pages ask for a card, not a password); `page.brand_on_hosting` (a brand page on S3, Blogspot, a PaaS or dynamic DNS before any form appears); `rules/page/brands.toml` (commonly impersonated brands missing from the URL list); more hosting platforms and dynamic DNS. The test half was not looked at before the final numbers.
- **Not added: certificate age.** 20 of 72 dev phishing pages had a certificate under 2 days old, but legitimate sites renew certificates every few weeks, so on its own it cannot separate them; it needs the domain's first certificate (certificate transparency) or registration date, which is D1b.
- The harness opens up to 5 pages at once (`--parallel`); the product keeps 3.

## Results (2026-11-11, `docs/eval/detonate.md`)

1,100 pages opened in the container: today's OpenPhish feed and the newest PhishTank entries (400), brand home pages and a Tranco top-10,000 sample (400), and `/login` on another Tranco sample (300). URLs were split by a fixed hash into a dev half (the only one looked at while changing rules) and a test half (reported). Wilson 95% intervals.

| Test half | Result |
|---|---|
| Phishing pages still alive when opened | 52.9% (118 of 223) |
| Caught, URL rules alone (alive) | 16.9% (11.2 to 24.7) |
| **Caught, URL rules + page (alive)** | **37.3% (29.1 to 46.3)**, 44 of 118; 24 only because of the page |
| MALICIOUS (alive) | 9.3% |
| Legitimate pages flagged, URL + page (alive) | 3.8% (7 of 186): 6 by the URL rules alone, 1 added by the page |
| Legitimate pages with a password form, flagged | 1 of 26 |
| Median time per page | about 5 s |

Dev half for comparison: phishing 33.6% with the page, legitimate 2.7%. **The first version of the page rules flagged 14% of legitimate dev pages** (logos read as brand claims, the word "devtools" in React builds, a newsletter box read as a sign-in page); those were fixed on the dev half before the test half was scored.

**Acceptance criteria:** 1 to 8 met (8: all suites, see the commit). Not met by design and stated: the page check is on request only (D1b).

**What the misses are:** pages with no brand in the title and no form (the form comes one step later), cloaked pages (a bot check, an empty page, a 403 to the scanner), brands not on the list, and generic "member login" kits on brand-new domains, which only domain age or certificate transparency can catch (D1b).
