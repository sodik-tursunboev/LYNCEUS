## 2. What DA4 measured today (`docs/eval/tld.md`, `fresh.md`, `evasion.md`, `drift.md`)

1. **The cheap ending is the lever a phisher can pull for free.** The domain-name model alone catches 49.4% of test phishing as things stand and **17.0% once the same names move to `.com`**. A model that sees phishing names under `.com` as well catches 46.2% as things stand (−3.2 points, legitimate flagged 0.46% against 0.38%) and **32.6% after the move (+15.6)**. Removing the ending from the model altogether catches 30.0% either way. *Trade: 3.2 points of today's catch for resilience against the one evasion that costs nothing.* **A two-model variant** (the shipped model and the `.com`-trained one, each at half the false-alarm budget, flagged if either flags) is the better trade: **47.7% as things stand (−1.7), 28.1% after the move to `.com` (+11.1), legitimate flagged 168 of 47,307 (0.36%, a hair below the shipped 0.38%)**. **Decision 1 below.**
2. **No sign of decay in 12 days.** Fresh feeds of 22 November, domains E1 never held: the whole engine flags 58.0% of 1,247 PhishTank and 88.3% of 282 OpenPhish addresses (these are newly listed names, so not like-for-like with E1's mixed-age test split, but nothing fell). Refitting the model with the fresh train part: fresh domains 61.3% to 63.4% (+2.1, intervals overlap), E1 test unchanged, false alarms unchanged.
3. **Remembering past feed listings per domain is worth little.** Of 1,447 newly listed PhishTank addresses only 91 (6%) sit on a domain listed twelve days earlier, and 79 more sit on a domain E1 holds as *legitimate*. A "this domain was listed before" rule would catch a few and would need decay and protection of shared hosts. Dropped from the ranking.
4. **Windows CI.** A clean Windows clone passes install, lint and all tests (2,879 passed, 59 skipped). GitHub's Windows job still fails in "Python lint and tests", most likely in the sandbox tests my replay skipped; its log needs a GitHub sign-in. It does not gate anything (continue-on-error). **Decision 9.**

## 3. The plan: five phases, each ending in a ledger of gates that can fail

Every change is judged on **paired counts over identical sets** with a **loss budget fixed before the change**, each lost address listed: E1 test (PhiUSIIL 11,347 of 30,090; PhishTank 3,720 of 14,075; OpenPhish 65 of 92), D1 test half (66 of 118), D1 live unseen (222 of 361), mail corpus (TP 73, FP 0), own browsing (0 of 92), and a fresh deep-link draw (the test half was read twice). Tune on E1 train and D1 dev only. Ledger `GATES-DA5.md`, approved with `--timeout 1800` as before.

### Phase 0: measure first, change nothing (about a day)
- **0a. Real hits on this machine.** Counts per rule from the verdicts since 16 November, to see what actually reaches the owner. *Needs the database, which is down (Docker): decision 8.*
- **0b. Classify the sandbox's refusals** (takedown, challenge page, sandbox fingerprint, empty stub) from the kept reports. Free; it decides whether cloaking work (Phase 3) is worth anything. The literature's rescue rate for switching profiles is only 3.74% against our 55% refusals *(review)*, so most refusals are probably dead pages.
- **0c. Keep daily snapshots of the two public feeds** from now on (public data, not the owner's), so "listed later than clicked" and recurrence can be measured instead of guessed. *Decision 2.*
- **0d. How many of the page-only catches pass `worth_opening`** (the automatic opener's filter), from `eval.detonate`.

### Phase 1: the deeper analysis tool and the cheap sure wins
- **1a. `python -m core.cli investigate <url|file|eml>` and a console "Investigate" tab: the evidence dossier.** Stages, most reuse what exists: (1) anatomy (parser, 20 rules, learned model with the tokens that pushed it, popular list); (2) redirect chain with per-hop host, address, status and certificate (the guarded resolver is built but unwired); (3) domain age and the visit's certificate; (4) the fetch, keeping the refusal status and body class and a second profile; (5) page structure (forms, frames, endpoints, plus a DOM-tree hash and the script's API set); (6) brand claim (text now, logo in Phase 2); (7) kit fingerprint (Phase 4); (8) intel; (9) **attribution: each signal's contribution to the score and the smallest counterfactuals that would flip the verdict** (drop a signal, an older domain, a brand-owned domain), computed by re-scoring with the published noisy-OR formula, no machine learning; (10) the optional brain's reading, labelled as advice. Output JSON and an HTML dossier. v1 = stages 1 to 5, 8 to 10, all existing parts plus attribution; it is also the instrument that makes every later false-alarm and detection decision legible. Gate: every stage has a fixture; the dossier of each E1/D1 fixture reproduces the engine's own verdict and score exactly; no dossier field names more than the stored evidence allows.
- **1b. Deliver the page verdict to the open tab.** The sandbox takes 5 to 10 seconds, but the extension caches UNKNOWN for 5 minutes and never asks again, so a deeper verdict reaches only "the next check". A "deeper look pending" flag and one re-ask at about 12 seconds replace the tab only when graver. Ceiling +2.2 to +4.0 points of live pages *(review: 130 to 139 of 223; 147 to 151 of 183)*. Verdicts are unchanged; they arrive in time. Gate: an end-to-end test with a fake detonator.
- **1c. Ranked sites count as established for the learned rule only.** The learned model alone accounts for up to 69 of 72 warned long-tail home pages; counting Tranco ranks 100,001 to 1M as established for that rule costs at most 80 of 14,360 model-flagged phishing addresses (0.56%, DA1). Loss budget: 80. Guard against bought ranks with a second Tranco snapshot 30+ days apart.
- **1d. Narrow loose conditions, each costed on E1 train first:** redirect_uri from official sign-in hosts weighs 0.4; credential_bait ignores "oauth" and long article slugs; refit weights with the deep links as legitimate data (E1's fit had 84). Ceiling: up to 7 of dev's 19 warned. Budget: 0 phishing lost (whole-word login words were rejected for losing 183 of 558 hits).
- **1e. Scheduled refit of the learned model from fresh feeds** (`eval.fresh` already measures it): protects up to about 13 points of drift. Adopt only if long-tail and legitimate rise by no more than 0.3 points and browsing stays 0 of 92. The cheap-ending variant joins only through decision 1.

### Phase 2: a new detector, aimed at the 91% the address cannot see
- **2a. Logo plus credential-intent brand match** (reference-based, Phishpedia/PhishIntention family, run in the existing container): the strongest published false-alarm evidence (86.5% fewer false alerts than the best baseline; 0.41% false-positive rate in an independent test) and it reads image-only shared-platform pages and ordinary-domain pages the text rules miss. Honest limits: modest recall alone (27.9% to 35.2% in that independent test), logo removal evades it, and only pages that load can be read (24.6% alive in our sample). It adds a dependency and model weights: decision 7. Gate: new live D1 run, test half read once; page-only catches rise; legitimate flagged at most 1 of 186; the matched reference logo is shown beside the page crop.
- **2b. Outbound links of platform pages.** Docs/Weebly/IPFS shells are 16.5% of misses (about 10.9% of E1 test phishing). The detonator keeps no anchors and the opener skips popular hosts, so an image with a link is never read. Capture the anchors, judge them with the URL engine and the redirect chain from 1a. Ceiling +10.9 points; realistic gain unknown (ordinary shared documents are everywhere). Gate: at least 60 live platform pages, test half read once; 0 flagged in a legitimate-shares sample.

### Phase 3: the agent gets intelligent (ordered by value over risk, *review*)
1. **Re-check queue.** On a feed update, diff old and new, match new hosts to stored page, download-origin and mail-link hosts (kept as keyed hashes, 30 days), raise one "retro" event: "yesterday's click is listed today". List membership decides; idempotent, audited, old verdicts untouched. Gate: `eval.retro` prints RETRO GATE MET (one alert across reruns, zero on the 1,640 deep links).
2. **Incident view.** Chain mail link, click, download and later program start by shared URL hash, host tag, file hash and time window, every edge labelled; a downloaded file's origin reads that host's stored verdict instead of re-deriving it from a bare address. Read-only; chain severity scored in its own category; no verdict edited. Gate: every scripted chain linked, zero incidents from benign-only seeds.
3. **Sentence composer.** What, why, how sure, what next, from signals, calibration and chain; the brain may only reword supplied facts. Gate: every rule id has a sentence; fake-model injection adds no fact.
4. **Confidence-aware asking.** Measure how often each score and confidence band was right on held-out halves (`calibration.toml`); an ask table (state, calibrated confidence, familiarity, source) chooses interrupt, banner, log or one targeted question. Only loudness changes; auto never touches mail or SUSPICIOUS. Gate: calibration error under 0.05; no two-category MALICIOUS quieted.
5. **Per-user memory and feedback loop.** A sealed per-domain table (first seen, distinct days, typed versus linked, labels). Two false-alarm labels on a site with no list, page, age or path evidence soften only the response for 30 days; rule labels become reviewed scoring diffs. Never touches a verdict. Gate: zero phishing softened on D1/E1 under adversarial labels. *Decision 3.*
6. **Program-start sensor** so the chain reaches the program that was run. Per-user polling (no admin) or ETW (changes ADR-016). Gate: 20 of 20 scripted launches seen. *Decision 5.*

### Phase 4: heavier, later
- Per-URL reputation as a second opinion (Safe Browsing hash-prefix lists, VirusTotal by URL hash): path-level knowledge for hacked sites and shared platforms. A new source and a change in what leaves the machine: decision 6. Collect two snapshots 24 hours apart first.
- Kit fingerprint library (script API-set clustering: same-family similarity 98.6% against 15.9% across families *(review)*; false alarms unpublished, so tested on our legitimate pages first).
- Retrain with tail negatives (after 1c shows what remains); files measured on this machine's own Downloads (installers were the only real-use false alarms *(review)*); mail corpora before linking Gmail (click-tracking targets, softfail below 0.45); VirusTotal domain tier measured on the RDAP samples; page-confirmed warning for single-category address-only flags (changes protection, so decision 4).

### Dropped, by the evidence
Domain memory of past feed listings (ceiling about 6% of new addresses, with legitimate-host risk); certificate age as a signal (0 of 118 phishing pages); certificate-transparency and passive-DNS classifiers (best published TPR 1.7% at 0.1% FPR, and 62 to 73% of phishing is on compromised hosts they cannot flag); favicon hashing (swappable, no accuracy figure); an LLM as the first line (published false-positive rates of 1.2% to 39.5%, 28.7% missed by one open model, prompt injection succeeds on HTML 64.7% to 84.7% of the time against open models) — it stays advisory; per-rule thresholds tuned on 92 history URLs (too few).

## 6. Results so far (2026-11-22)

**Measured, no behaviour change (Phase 0b, 0d)** (`docs/eval/refusals.md`, `python -m eval.refusals`). Of the phishing pages that answered the sandbox with a refusal, most are **takedowns, not cloaking**: in the DA1 run 101 answered HTTP 403 and 90 of those are a hosting provider's abuse notice or the sandbox's own proxy refusal; in D1 60 of 65. Blocks and challenge pages together are 5.8% (3.6 to 9.2) of D1 and 1.5% (0.6 to 3.4) of DA1 phishing pages, against 15.7% and 18.2% of legitimate pages, so a cloaking-aware fetch has a small ceiling and stays last. **The automatic opener's filter is the bigger lever:** of the pages only the page rules caught, just 1 of 15 (D1) and 2 of 8 (DA1) would have been opened automatically by the product (18 of the 23 are UNKNOWN with no sign, 2 are on a popular host), so most of the page's measured gain never reaches the person. Widening what is opened by itself costs sandbox visits to unknown sites (the site sees this computer's address), so it is an owner decision and nothing was changed.

**1b built:** the deeper look reaches the tab. The core says when an automatic opening is running (`deeper` on `/v1/urls/analyze`, 2 to 12 s), the agent passes it through (382 agent tests, 12 new), the extension asks once more and replaces the tab only when the new verdict is graver (119 extension tests, 4 new); no manifest change. Real-Edge end-to-end not run.

**1c and 1d adopted, inside the budgets fixed first** (`docs/eval/da5-rules.md`, `python -m eval.da5_accept` prints DA5 ACCEPTANCE MET): the learned model is silent for Tranco ranks 100,001 to 1,000,000 (a compact 7 MB hash table written by the existing `popular sync`; the file for this machine was generated offline from the E1 list); a sign-in service's `redirect_uri` is `url.idp_redirect` at weight 0.3 instead of an open redirect at 0.5; `auth` inside `oauth` is no login word (ruleset 1.5.0). Cost: **77 of 15,132 flagged E1 test phishing addresses lost (0.51%, budget 85)**, all from the ranked tier, none from 1d; D1 test half unchanged at 66 of 118 caught and 1 of 186 legitimate flagged; own browsing 0 of 97. Gain: deep-link dev flags 19 to 13, test 20 to 10, long tail 72 to 3 of 3,000 (**partly circular**: the sample is drawn from the same list). The lost addresses are in `.data/eval/da5/lost.jsonl`.

**3.3 composer built:** every rule has a plain sentence (`core/explain/phrases.toml`, 137 rules) and the URL engine's explanation now says what, why, how sure, what next; a model may only reword and is rejected if it adds a fact (`reword` is not wired to any model). The email and file engines still use the old wording.

**3.4 calibration and ask table built, not wired** (`docs/eval/calibration.md`): per score band the detection and false-alarm rates measured on a fitting half reproduce on the held-out half within 0.05 (largest gap 0.036). **Caveat the report states:** E1's split hashes the whole address, so 47% of held-out phishing shares a registered domain with the fitting half; with repeat domains removed four E1 bands drift by 0.17 to 0.19. Treat the bands as stable for known domains only. `core/risk/asktable.py` encodes the hard rules (a two-category MALICIOUS is never quieter than a banner; auto never touches mail or SUSPICIOUS; UNKNOWN is never silent) but no consumer uses it yet.

**1e adopted: learned model 3** (`docs/eval/refit.md`, `python -m eval.refit`; `eval.da5_accept` checks the criteria and prints DA5 ACCEPTANCE MET). Fitted on E1's train split plus 770 train-part phishing domains from the feeds of 22 November, threshold at 0.4% of E1's calibration legitimate names (0.9908). In the whole engine against the model it replaced: E1 test phishing flagged +292 addresses (OpenPhish 64 to 66 of 92, PhishTank 3,704 to 3,778 of 14,075, PhiUSIIL 11,287 to 11,503 of 30,090); fresh test domains 64.6% to 67.0% (297 to 308 of 460); legitimate flagged on PhiUSIIL 44 to 53 of 40,685 (0.11% to 0.13%); deep-link dev 13 to 14, test 10 to 10; long tail 3 to 3; browsing 0 of 97. **D1 test half with it: 67 of 118 caught (was 66), 1 of 186 legitimate flagged.** The model it replaced is kept in `.data/eval/da5/model2` so `eval.da5_rules` stays reproducible. `docs/eval/learned-url.md` still describes model 2. A refit is now one command; it should be repeated every few months on fresh feeds (decision 2 about daily snapshots would give it more data).

**1a built: `python -m core.cli investigate <url | .eml | file> [--json] [--html FILE] [--page-report FILE]`** (`docs/INVESTIGATE.md`, offline, opens no socket). One dossier with the same sections for a link, a message or a file: anatomy, the learned model's reading with the tokens that pushed it, established-site status, redirect chain, domain, fetch, page (from a saved page report), brand, intel, **attribution** (per signal and per category, rebuilding the score exactly: signal points sum to the risk), **counterfactuals** (the fewest signals whose removal drops below SUSPICIOUS or MALICIOUS, found by exhaustive search and confirmed by the engine's own scorer) and limits. Stages that need the network or Docker say "not asked (offline)"; the live redirect, registry and sandbox stages are the next version, and there is no console tab yet. A 180-message and 199-URL sweep found 0 attribution mismatches.

**3.1 re-check queue built and wired** (`docs/eval/retro.md`, `python -m eval.retro check` prints RETRO GATE MET): after a feed update the core diffs what the update added, matches it to stored links (URL hash, and a bare host tag) from the last 30 days, and raises at most one `retro_alerts` row per link and feed plus an audit line; it never edits an old verdict and ignores popular and official hosts. Migration 0007. Limits: a page URL stored with a `#fragment` never matches by hash (host matching still works); the listing time is the update that first carried the entry; the agent's toast and a console view are not built.

**3.2 incident view built, origin wired** (`docs/eval/incidents.md`, `python -m eval.incidents check` prints INCIDENT GATE MET): `core/incidents` links a mail link, the same URL, a download from that host and a repeated file hash by shared keys with every edge labelled, read-only, severity in its own `chain` category, never a verdict edit; 8 scripted chains found with 20 labelled edges, 6 benign-only timelines and 6 decoy timelines make no incident. A downloaded file's origin now also reads the host's stored URL verdict and raises the same `file.origin_flagged` signal when that verdict was SUSPICIOUS or MALICIOUS (it only adds, never cancels the address check). The time windows (30 minutes, 7 days, fan-out 10) and the chain severity constants are my choices, not measured. No console view, no API route, no program-start events yet.

**Not done, by decision or Docker** (ledger G80 to G88): real per-rule hits on this machine, feed snapshots, logo matching, outbound links of platform pages, the familiarity table, the program-start sensor, per-URL reputation and kit fingerprints, page-confirmed warnings, the Windows CI log, and the cheap-ending variant of the learned model (the two-model variant would cost 1.7 points of today's catch and gain 11.1 after a move to `.com`).
